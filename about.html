How I think about product

Three things I keep coming back to, whether I'm building a feature spec or cleaning a dataset:

1. The user's actual behavior is the source of truth.

Not what they say in a survey, not what a stakeholder wishes were true. When a redemption pattern in the data contradicts the story the fund team is telling, the data wins. When a student can't find the submit button, "the button is right there" is not the answer.

2. Constraints are the interesting part.

Anyone can design for a blank slate. The work is usually reconciling things that look mutually exclusive: administrative compliance and student UX, model accuracy and speed, what's clever and what's shippable. I like sitting in that trade-off and finding the version that gives up the least on both sides.

3. Ship the small thing first.

A rough version in front of a real user teaches you more in a day than a month of internal debate. Most of my strongest decisions started as an okay first draft I was willing to throw out.
