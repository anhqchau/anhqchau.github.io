Deciding what AI is actually worth adopting inside a law firm.

RoleInnovation Intern
TeamFirm Innovation / Practice Support
TimelineMar 2026–Present
FocusTool evaluation, pilot design, adoption

Problem

The AI-for-legal space is loud. Every vendor claims a 10x productivity lift, every partner has read a different article, and every associate has a slightly different daily workflow. The firm needed a way to separate real signal from marketing before committing to any tool at scale — and a way to pilot the ones that survived that filter without disrupting billable work.

Approach

  • Built an evaluation rubric that scored tools on accuracy in Vietnamese and English legal contexts, integration with the firm's existing case management system, data residency (a real constraint for VN legal practice), and lawyer-time saved per document type.
  • Ran hands-on pilots with associates across [TODO: N] practice groups, structured so we captured before/after time on a matched set of tasks.
  • Wrote the internal recommendation memo — one page of decision, plus an appendix nobody would read unless they doubted the decision.

Impact

The work is ongoing. [TODO: fill in adopted tools, pilots completed, time-saved metrics from any concluded pilot.]

What I took away

"Should we adopt this AI tool" is not a technical question. It's a product management question: what job is this actually being hired for, who does it change the workflow of, and what does success look like six months in when the novelty is gone? The firms that skip that step buy expensive shelfware.
